Ask once, not twenty times

The next plan to move onto the new flow came with a long health questionnaire. With up to 20 people per policy, the problem was multiplication.

Role
Product Designer (UX/UI), Indra Group
Timeline
Jan to Jul 2026
Team
Client UX, PM, functional analyst, engineering and business
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Excel
Status
Delivered to development, not in production

Problem

Each person on the policy answered the full questionnaire separately. The flow had to collect the same and be much shorter.

What I did

Inverted the question model, branched the questionnaire, redesigned review and editing, and proposed the design of the verification and payment-recovery emails.

Outcome

My model became the final one, over an easier-to-build infinite scroll. One design serves two plans.

01Context

Same flow, much heavier form

This plan required a health declaration: 31 conditional questions on general health, habits and specialties. A policy could cover up to 20 people, and each answered everything alone. The cost grew with every person, exactly the people the business most wants to enroll.

02The idea

Flip the question

Before · per person

“Has Jane been hospitalized?” “Has Joe?” “Has Joe Senior?”, for every question and every person.

After · per question

“Has anyone on the policy been hospitalized?” Yes → “Select who.” Anyone not selected is a No, and details are asked only for the yeses.

The common case, healthy people, gets the shortest path: one answer covers the whole family. I also redesigned the review: a summary in accordions by specialty with inline editing, so nobody starts over.

03The hard call

Easier to build versus easier to finish

On the table was an infinite scroll with one questionnaire per person, simpler to build. I argued with two kinds of evidence:

  1. A 35-page benchmark of long forms, especially on mobile: progress indicators from N26, Oscar and Stripe Atlas, and criteria for multi-step versus single page.
  2. The arithmetic of answers saved: on a 20-person policy, 31 instead of 620.

The inverted model became the final one.

04Beyond the screen

Part of the flow happens in the inbox

The code, a rejected payment or the reminder to finish arrive by email. The business gave me the content and I proposed the design, in desktop and responsive and by process state. The 48-hour window was set by the business.

05Outcome

One design, two plans

A sibling plan had no prototypes of its own for lack of time, but they’re similar enough that one development serves both.

Honest statusIn development when I left, no production data. This project had no test of its own: the research numbers come from the previous case.
